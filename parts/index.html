Reliefbay scan-processor relief: 96 scanlines of the real Santa Catalina elevation grid, each lifted by height and drawn as double-stroked phosphor wire; hidden lines removed by filling each line's curtain (the vertical strip from the wire down to the base) with the field colour, far to near, the Unknown Pleasures way the Rutt/Etra scan processor (Steve Rutt and Bill Etra, 1973), which bent each video scanline by its brightness; the CP 1919 pulsar stack (Harold Craft, 1970) that became the Unknown Pleasures cover (1979) 13.8 KB 1.6 to 2.0 ms/frame on the parts counter @ 1440x900 x1 internal, headless Chromium (2026-09-30); 390x844 at 4x CPU (lite path): 8.0 to 8.4 ms JS, part page 44 to 47 fps; JS time only, not a real-GPU frame time
Terminatorbay a back-culled dotted sphere in orthographic projection with faint graticule, land drawn as denser and brighter dots from a 1-degree land mask, lit by a Lambert term from the real sub-solar point so the day line sits where it sits right now Elite's dotted planets (David Braben and Ian Bell, Acornsoft, 1984); the IRIX-era wireframe globe (Silicon Graphics workstations, early 1990s) 16.5 KB 0.67 ms per full redraw @ 2160x1350 internal (1440x900 x1.5), 0.52 ms/frame on the parts counter while dragging, 0.01 ms/frame idle (it redraws only on change); headless Chromium, software raster (2026-09-29); phone TBD
