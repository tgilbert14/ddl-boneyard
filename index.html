A Desert Data Labs experience

Boneyard

Eight retired display machines, tubes still on, in a row on the desert floor. Scroll to fly the row. Pull any part.

A fiction of display machines, not the aircraft at Davis-Monthan. The clock in the corner shows your local time, and the horizon colour follows your hour.

01Jump

Hyperspace starfield · Star Wars 1977 · the 1/z starfields of the early 1990s · Windows 3.1 Starfield Simulation 1992

The stars you were resting under begin to streak. Hold the pointer, a finger or Space to raise warp; let go and it settles.

02Gate

Slit-scan · Douglas Trumbull, 2001: A Space Odyssey, 1968

One column of a real Sonoran sunset, stretched into a corridor. Only that one column of pixels ships with the page.

03Range

Voxel Space · NovaLogic, Comanche: Maximum Overkill, 1992

A flight over the real Santa Catalinas: SRTM and USGS 3DEP elevation in 64 m cells, drawn with 2x vertical exaggeration.

04Wash

Vector-field particle advection · scientific visualization, 1990s

Rain finds the washes on the same mountains you just flew. The drops follow the downhill slope of the ground. It is a slope model, not hydrology.

05Relief

Rutt/Etra scan processor · Steve Rutt and Bill Etra, 1973 · CP 1919 plot, Harold Craft, 1970

The range you flew and watered, held as an object: scanlines bent by elevation, turning slowly until you drag it.

06Terminator

Dotted planets · Elite, David Braben and Ian Bell, 1984

A dotted globe with today's day line, computed from your clock. Tucson in amber. Drag to spin; it comes back to face Tucson.

07Scope

Oscilloscope XY mode · Lissajous figures, Jules Antoine Lissajous, 1857

Hold the pointer or Space to hear two tones; the trace then draws the actual audio signal, left channel across and right channel up. Nothing on this row makes a sound unless you hold it down.

08Mark

Glenz vectors · the Amiga scene, about 1989 to 1990

The Desert Data Labs mark as a see-through solid, the same glyph that stood as every pylon on the way in. Drag to turn it.

That is the row.

Every screen you flew past is one plain file: a provenance header, a PARAMS block, one mount() function. The shelf holds all of them.

Elevation: AWS Terrain Tiles (Mapzen Terrarium), compiled from SRTM, USGS 3DEP and others. Life-zone bands from the Desert Data Labs 27 Miles project.
